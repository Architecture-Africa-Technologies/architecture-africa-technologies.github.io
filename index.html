HOW IT WORKS

Simple. Directed. Automatic.

One standing customer instruction. A seven-stage journey that leaves the original eligible payment independent.

1

Select either a fixed KES amount or a percentage, plus an eligible destination.

2

Authorize an eligible payment channel through agreed provider interfaces.

3

Transact

Make an everyday payment normally through your provider.

4

Capture

Initiate the separate, customer-authorized additional amount.

5

Redirect

Authorized institutions route funds toward the chosen destination.

6

Inform

Confirm the outcome after the appropriate receiving-institution response.

7

Track

Keep a capture footprint; check actual balances with the account provider.

Three institutions of trust. Three distinct messages.

The originator can confirm the payment; the receiver can confirm its own credit; BOUNCE can send its capture confirmation last. Each party retains its existing customer relationship and notification practices.

Original payments remain independent.

If a separate capture cannot proceed due to insufficient funds, the original eligible payment is not cancelled by BOUNCE. Initial outcomes shown in the prototype are Successful or Failed, not invented settlement states.

Technical questions we expect a serious partner to ask

Who moves and holds funds?
Only licensed or otherwise authorized institutions in their agreed roles. BOUNCE coordinates customer instructions, capture outcomes and reference data. It does not serve as a wallet, account, money-holder or balance authority.
How does the receiving institution confirm?
A partner-defined acknowledgment, signed event, status query or equivalent interface confirms the relevant state. An instruction's acceptance alone must never be labeled a credited destination. Matching reference and reconciliation rules are agreed per rail.
How are interruptions and duplicate events handled?
Proposed controls include idempotency identifiers, replay-safe event handling, audit trails, conflict resolution, retry discipline and explicit failure reporting. Detailed implementation requires partner technical design and testing.
Can three SMS messages reach the customer?
Yes, where providers and customer preferences permit: the original payment provider, the receiving financial institution and BOUNCE each communicate a distinct fact. BOUNCE can arrive last once the proper destination confirmation is received.
Can the institution display its products?
With formal participation and approvals, an institution could display verified offers, conditions and an authorized registration link. The demonstration directory is illustrative and does not yet represent these storefront connections.
What about account balances and real-time guarantees?
Actual balances remain the institution's records. Authorized APIs could make provider-reported values visible in BOUNCE. Low-latency confirmation is a design objective to be measured during partner tests, not a guaranteed number of seconds.

Five principles behind the platform

Open any highlight to explore the principle in more detail.

🇰🇪 Kenya-first
The demonstration is grounded in KES, familiar Kenyan channels and potential financial destinations, with future expansion subject to market-specific readiness.
👥 Customer choice
Exactly one rule method per instruction: a fixed KES amount or a percentage of the original eligible payment, with a chosen receiving destination and customer control.
🛡 Non-custodial design
BOUNCE holds no funds. Providers execute fund movements and keep their accounts, ledgers, customer relationships and balance records. Original payments remain independent.
🔗 Partner-led integration
Agree consent, eligibility, security, event mapping, capture routing, receiving-institution confirmation and reconciliation before a controlled live pilot.
🏦 Institutional independence
Each participating institution retains its customers, branding, products, records, approvals and notification rights. BOUNCE adds a capability without taking over an institution.
